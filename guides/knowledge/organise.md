# Pages inside pages, and finding things

A handbook is useful when people can find the page they need. Three things do that: the tree of pages, topics, and
search.

## Pages inside a page

An article's [subtasks](/everyday/subtasks) are the pages inside it. Each one is a card of its own, with its own
text, pictures, owner and comments, and it can have pages inside it too.

Use them when an article has parts people read separately:

- "Your first week" with a page for each day,
- "How we quote a job" with a page for kitchens, one for bathrooms, one for roofs,
- a policy with a page of questions people ask about it.

To add one, open the article and click **Add a subtask**, or point at its row in the Outline and click the **+**.
Open the new card and write in its description, like any article.

Keep the outer article short: say what the pages inside are for, and who to ask. Its text is the introduction.

## The Outline is the table of contents

Open the **Outline** tab. Every article is a row, with the pages inside it indented under it.

![The handbook in the Outline, grouped by topic](/images/km-2-outline.webp)

- **Fold and unfold** an article with the arrow beside it. The two small buttons in the **Task** heading fold or
  unfold everything.
- **Group by topic.** Click **Display → Group by → Topic**. The handbook is now laid out by subject, with a
  heading for each. Group by **Owner** to see what each person keeps, or by **List** to see what is in review.
- **Read one part.** Point at an article that has pages inside it and click **Focus on its subtasks**. The Outline
  shows only that part.
- **Sort by Review by**, by clicking the column's heading, to see what is due a second look.
- **Reorder** by dragging rows, when the Outline isn't grouped or sorted. The order is the handbook's reading
  order.

Columns for **Owner**, **Review by** and **Topic** are there because they are the board's fields. Hide the columns
you don't need under **Display**.

See [the Outline view](/views/outline) for everything it does.

## Find an article

**By its title:** click the search icon at the top of the board and type. The board and the Outline narrow down as
you type.

**By words inside it:** use [Search cards](/everyday/search-and-filters#search-cards-every-board-at-once). It looks
through titles, descriptions and comments.

![Search cards finding a word inside the handbook's articles](/images/km-5-search.webp)

1. Click your picture at the top right, then **Search all cards**.
2. Choose the handbook where it says **All boards**, so only its articles are searched.
3. Type the words. A result found through a comment shows the line they were in.

Search cards also finds archived articles, so an outdated page that was put away can still be read.

**By what it is:** click **Filter** on the board for a label ("policy"), an owner, or a topic. Save a combination
you use often as a [preset](/everyday/search-and-filters#presets-save-a-combination).

## Link articles to each other, and to the work

- **From one article to another:** type `/`, choose **Card**, and pick the other article. Its name becomes a link.
- **From a task to an article:** do the same in the task's description or in a comment, on any board. "Quoted per
  HB-7" in a job's card takes the next person straight to how you quote.
- **To find what mentions an article:** search for its name (HB-7) in Search cards.

For a link that both cards show, add a [card link field](/everyday/fields#link-cards-together), for example
"Related articles".

## Good to know

- To put a page inside another article, drag its row in the Outline, or click **Change parent** at the top of the
  card.
- An article can be [moved to another board](/everyday/cards) with its pages, its comments and its files.
- The Outline shows the first 500 rows of a big board, with **Show more**.
