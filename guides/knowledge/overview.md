# A knowledge base on a board

Kanbanto has no separate place for documents, and it doesn't need one. A board can be your team's handbook: every
card is an article, and the board shows where each article is in its life, from an idea to something everyone
follows.

![A handbook kept on a board: articles as cards, in lists from Inbox to Outdated](/images/km-1-board.webp)

This group of pages shows how to set one up, write in it, keep it in order and keep it true. Nothing here is a
special mode: it is an ordinary board, used a particular way.

## How the pieces fit

| A knowledge base needs | In Kanbanto it is |
|---|---|
| An article | A card. Its title is the article's title. |
| The article's text | The card's [description](/everyday/descriptions): headings, lists, tables, checklists and pictures. |
| A page to read it on | **Expand**, above the description: the text full page, with its contents on the side. |
| Pages inside a page | [Subtasks](/everyday/subtasks). An article's subtasks are the pages inside it, as deep as you need. |
| A table of contents | The [Outline](/views/outline): every article with the pages inside it, to fold, group and filter. |
| Draft, in review, published, outdated | The board's lists. |
| An owner, and a day to look at it again | Two [fields of your own](/everyday/fields). |
| Tags and topics | Labels, and a choice field. |
| Finding it again | [Search cards](/everyday/search-and-filters#search-cards-every-board-at-once), which looks inside the text. |
| Readers who shouldn't change anything | People you [share the board with](/people/sharing) as viewers, or anyone with its public link. |
| Your assistant | [Connected](/ai/connect), it reads and searches the articles like any other card. |

## Why keep it beside the work

- **It gets written.** The lesson from a job is one click away from the job's card. Mention the article in the
  task, or the task in the article, and each links to the other.
- **It gets reviewed.** An article waiting for a second pair of eyes is a card in a list, with a name on it. Nobody
  has to remember.
- **It doesn't rot unseen.** A date on each article, and a filter for the ones that are past it, show what to look
  at again.

## Is this for you?

It suits a team that already works in Kanbanto and wants what it learns kept in the same place: a handbook, how-tos,
decisions and why they were made, answers to the questions customers keep asking. A few hundred articles sit
comfortably on one board.

It isn't a publishing tool. If you need thousands of pages, a different set of readers for every page, or a public
documentation site with its own design, use something made for that.
