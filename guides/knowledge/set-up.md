# Set up the board

A knowledge board is an ordinary board with five lists, three fields and a few labels. This takes about ten
minutes, and only the first two steps are needed to start writing.

## 1. Make the board

On your boards page, click **Create board**, choose **An empty board**, and give it a name people will look for:
"Team handbook", "How we work", "Support answers".

Say what it is for in the board's description (**⋯ → Board settings**). New people read it first.

## 2. Make the lists

The lists are an article's life. Rename the three a new board has, and add two:

| List | Counts as | What is in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Inbox** | Not started | Something worth writing down. A title is enough. |
| **Drafting** | In progress | Someone is writing it. |
| **In review** | In progress | Written, waiting for a second pair of eyes. |
| **Published** | **In progress** | It is what we do. |
| **Outdated** | Done | It was true once. |

Set what each list counts as from its menu: **⋯ → Cards in this list are…**. See
[lists on the Board](/views/board#lists).

::: tip Published counts as In progress, not Done
A list for finished work shows only the cards finished in the last 14 days, and the Outline strikes their titles
through. That is right for a task and wrong for an article people should keep finding. A published article isn't
finished: it is in use. **Outdated** is the list that counts as Done, and there the fading is exactly what you want.
:::

A new board also has a hidden **Backlog** list. Leave it hidden, or delete it.

## 3. Add three fields

[Fields of your own](/everyday/fields) say what a card on this board is about. Make these three and put them on the
board (**⋯ → Board settings → Fields**):

| Field | Kind | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner** | A person | Who keeps the article right. Not the same as the assignee, who is whoever is writing or reviewing it now. |
| **Review by** | A date | The day someone should read it again and check it is still true. |
| **Topic** | A choice | The part of the handbook it belongs to: "Start here", "Customers", "Tools". |

Switch **On cards** on for **Topic**, so the Board says at a glance what each article is about.

Keeping the Owner apart from the assignee matters. A published article has an owner for years, and nobody working
on it. With the owner in a field, published articles stay out of everyone's "My tasks".

## 4. Add labels for the kind of article

Labels say what sort of thing an article is, across topics: **how-to**, **policy**, **decision**, **faq**. Make
them from any card's **Labels**.

## 5. Save an article to start from

Write one card with the headings you want every article to have, then **⋯ → Save as template…** and call it
"Article". From then on, the small arrow beside **Add a card** starts a new article with those headings. See
[templates](/everyday/templates), and the outlines in [samples to copy](/knowledge/samples).

## 6. Save two ways of looking

[Presets](/everyday/search-and-filters#presets-save-a-combination) keep a filter and a view under a name, for
everyone on the board.

- **Review overdue.** Click **Filter**, go down to **Fields**, pick **Review by**, and choose **In the past**. Save
  it as a preset. It lists the articles someone should read again.
- **By topic.** In the Outline, click **Display → Group by → Topic**, and save that as a preset. It is the
  handbook's table of contents.

## 7. Tell the reviewers

A [rule](/views/telling-rules) can tell people when an article is ready. Open the **In review** list's menu, choose
**Tell people when a card arrives…**, and tick the people who review. They get a line under their bell each time
an article arrives.

## 8. Share it

Open **Share** (see [share a board](/people/sharing)):

- **Editors** write and review.
- **Viewers** read everything and can comment, and can't change an article.
- **Anyone with the link can view** is a switch at the bottom. With it on, anyone who has the link reads the
  board without an account, and can't change or comment. Good for a handbook that new starters should see before
  their first day.
