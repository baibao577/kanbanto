# Write an article

An article is a card, and its text is the card's description. Everything on
[writing a description](/everyday/descriptions) applies. This page is about what makes a description read like a
document.

## Start one

Click **Add a card** in **Inbox** (an idea) or **Drafting** (you are writing it now). If the board has an
**Article** template, click the small arrow beside **Add a card** and start from it.

Give it a title someone would search for. A question or a task works best: "How we quote a job", "What to do when
a customer complains", "Why we stopped Saturday visits".

Assign it to yourself while you write. Fill in its **Topic** now, and its **Owner** and **Review by** when it is
published.

## Write the text

Open the card and click the description. Type `/` on a new line for headings, lists, a table, a checklist and the
rest.

- **Headings make the contents.** Each heading you add shows in the list on the left of the full page. Use them for
  the parts a reader would jump to.
- **Numbered lists for steps**, a **table** for anything with two sides to it (who to ask about what, a price for
  each kind of work), a **checklist** for things to tick off.
- **Other articles and tasks by name.** Type `/`, choose **Card**, and pick one. Its name (like HB-12) becomes a
  link, and stays one when the other card is renamed or moved.

Text written elsewhere, by you or by an assistant, can be pasted in: it keeps its headings and lists.

## Put pictures in

Drop a picture into the text, or paste a screenshot. It is attached to the card and **shown right there**, as wide
as the text allows.

![An article full page, with a picture in the text and its contents on the left](/images/km-3-page.webp)

- A picture on a line of its own is shown. Its name, with a paperclip in front of it, is what the text holds, so
  you can move the picture by moving that line.
- A picture named in the middle of a sentence stays a small link to click.
- To use a picture the card already has, type `#` and the start of its name on an empty line.
- Click a picture to open it at its full size.

Files that aren't pictures, such as a PDF or a spreadsheet, are always small links.

## Read and write it full page

Click **Expand** above the description. The article opens full page: the text in a reading column, and **Contents**
on the left.

- **Edit** starts writing where you were reading. **Done**, or <kbd>Esc</kbd>, finishes.
- **Copy link** copies a link that opens the article full page. Send that one when you want someone to read it:
  they land on the page, not on the card.

## Split a long article

A description holds about 8,000 words. Long before that, an article is easier to read as a few pages. Give it
[pages inside it](/knowledge/organise#pages-inside-a-page): one subtask for each part, each with its own text.

## When it is ready

Drag the card to **In review**. If the board has a rule on that list, the reviewers are told. See
[review and keep it current](/knowledge/review).

## Good to know

- Your writing is saved as you write, every few seconds. Someone else can open the same article and write with
  you: see [write together](/knowledge/together).
- An article keeps its earlier versions. **Expand**, then **Versions**.
- A reader can tick a checklist item without editing the article. Use that for checklists people work through,
  and plain bullet points for ones they only read.
- An assistant connected to Kanbanto can draft an article from your notes, and can read every article when you
  ask it a question. See [what to ask](/ai/what-to-ask).
